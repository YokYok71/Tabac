## Scale — four fixes, and the two halves of the list freeze

**AN ENTRY ANIMATION COULD BE SCHEDULED MINUTES OUT.** Every list computes its delay from the row index (`100 + idx * 50`), unbounded: MEASURED on a 5000-session journal the last card's timer fired at **250 seconds**, each row its own `setTimeout` + `setState` over a **165 118**-node tree — at 4x CPU throttle, 288 further long tasks averaging 122 ms, still going when observation stopped. `ENTER_MAX_DELAY_MS` (700) caps the DELAY **inside `useEnter`**, and that placement is the point: capping the index at each call site would cover the six current lists and no future one, whereas the rule — *no entry animation may begin later than this* — belongs to the animation. Nothing visible changes; past ~12 rows the stagger is off screen.

**THE LARGER HALF — every row in the DOM — IS NOW BOUNDED, and this paragraph used to say it was not fixed.** It read *"DISCLOSED, NOT FIXED: the lists are not windowed … 165 118 nodes for that journal, 201 029 for a 20 000-row catalogue"*, and those figures were close to the ones a fresh measurement produced (185 654 and 200 613). The flat branches render a growing prefix now — see `useProgressiveList` in the structure tree for the before/after table and for what is still NOT windowing. The correction is left in place rather than deleted because a note recording a deferral has to be closed by whoever resolves it: a stale to-do does not merely go useless, it points the next reader backwards.

**`computePipeUsageProfile` WAS QUADRATIC — the twin of a defect already fixed once.** It rebuilds its tobacco→category map AND rescans every session for EACH pipe, so calling it per pipe is O(pipes x sessions): **61.8 ms** at 200 x 5000, against **0.96 ms** for `computePipeRest` and **2.23 ms** for `computePipeMaintenanceReminders` on identical inputs — its two siblings in the same component, both of which hoist their index. `PipesListView`'s memo depends on all three `liveData` arrays, so it re-paid that **on every save, from every screen** (the view is mounted even while it returns null). `buildPipeCategoryIndex(sessions, tobaccos)` is the optional precomputed index, exactly the repair `pipeSessionsSinceMaint` was given after a consolidation dropped ITS hoist: one implementation, and single-pipe callers pass nothing and are unchanged.

**THE JOURNAL SORT PUT A LINEAR `.find` INSIDE THE COMPARATOR.** At 5000 sessions x 300 tobaccos, sorting by date is 6.2 ms and sorting by tobacco was **257 ms — 41x** — doubling on EITHER axis. `sortBy` is local state, but the view never unmounts, so once picked it re-paid on every save from any screen. A label `Map` is built once outside the comparator; `restMap` in the same file already had the shape. `tobOf`/`pipeOf` stay for the RENDER path, where they run once per visible row.

**THE CATALOGUE'S OWNED/WISHED SETS RE-RESOLVED THE WHOLE CELLAR ON EVERY SAVE.** Both memos were keyed on `data`, which changes identity on every save, while `db` is set on the first visit to that page and never cleared (the view self-gates rather than unmounting) — so after one visit this ran from the Home screen for the rest of the session. Cost is per MISS, and a miss falls through the full fuzzy ladder: **1 309 ms** at 300 tobaccos against a 20 000-blend catalogue, **145 ms** even against a 1 594-blend one. Keyed on the COLLECTION now (`tobList` / `wishList` hoisted so the dep is exact rather than omitted — the warning budget stays at 7).

**MEASURED AND FOUND FINE, so the next reader does not re-chase them:** every pure aggregator is linear and cheap at 300/200/100/5000 (`computeChartStats` 3.9 ms, `computeTasteProfile` 9.9, `computeAgingSweetSpot` 9.0, `computeAromaProfile` 8.6, `buildTobaccoAromaIndex` 6.3, `computeCostPerSession` 1.6, `computeWatchlist` 0.7, `duplicateCount` 1.4); `liveData` is a shallow filter+map, not a deep copy; `migrateData` on a 2.77 MB cellar is 36 ms, load-path only. **The engine layer is not the problem.** **CORRIGÉ EN PLACE, ET LA PÉREMPTION EST L'ENTRÉE.** Ce paragraphe se terminait par « *Still open and disclosed:* `appCtx` … the photo effect re-reads every `local-photo-*` from IndexedDB on every save (~63 ms for 300 covers) … and the catalogue search filters 20 000 rows per keystroke undebounced (83-134 ms each) » — **deux des trois étaient déjà réparés quand on est revenu les lire.** Le lecteur de photos saute désormais toute clé que la carte porte déjà (`imgLocalRef`, 0 lecture par sauvegarde contre 600), et la recherche du catalogue est débouncée à `CATALOG_SEARCH_DEBOUNCE_MS` (160 ms) — les deux ont leurs propres entrées plus haut dans ce fichier, écrites par la personne qui a fait le travail et jamais reportées ici. **C'est la panne que ce fichier nomme ailleurs : une note qui enregistre un report doit être refermée par celui qui la résout, sinon elle ne devient pas seulement inutile, elle pointe le lecteur suivant en arrière.** Trouvée en répondant à « il reste quoi ? ## Multi-device convergence, as a simulation

**The merge engine has been repaired seven times and every repair came from a hand-written scenario**, so the suite covered the cases somebody thought of. `multiDeviceConvergence.property.test.ts` generates the rest: three devices mounted with the REAL `useTobaccoStore` / `useSessionStore` / `useTrashOps` over a shared save box, ~20 ordinary operations each, then real `stageImport(…, {autoApply: "merge"})` merges — 120 fast-check schedules per property. Six properties, each with a non-vacuity counter asserted after `fc.assert`: `checkAllInvariants` empty after the merge AND after the round trip, no silent loss, no silent duplication without a counter, idempotency, order-independence, and exact lot balance in both directions. **All six held; no production defect.** Probed independently of the author's own five: making an appended lot take a FRESH `uid` instead of the imported one reddens 4 of the 10 cases.

**THE FIDELITY REVERSAL IS THE MOST USEFUL THING IN IT.** The first version reset `monotonicId`'s module counter at every device switch, on the stated grounds that without it the whole `lot-id-unique-global` class is unreachable from one test process. That produced **33 post-merge invariant failures, 24 post-round-trip, 86 `lot-id-unique-global` and 2 `lot-balance-overflow`** — every one MANUFACTURED: the reset restarts the id clock inside a single test millisecond, so the merge's re-stamp landed on an id a LOCAL lot already carried. Remove it and the same 120 schedules yield zero. **A harness knob that makes a rare class "reachable" can instead make it CERTAIN, and the failures it then produces are measurements of the knob.** Same family as the `_langUsable` pseudo-loc mode that was silently dead, and as the seed lessons: what the harness does to reach a state is part of what the run measures.

**The DESIGNED refusals are quantified, not changed.** Over 120 schedules: `trashedSkipped` 15, `identityConflicts` 8, `lotsTrashedSkipped` 3, `sessionsDetached` 2. Each is asserted with a **floor of 1, never a pinned figure** — the exact counts are a property of the generator's weights, so pinning them would fail on a harmless weight change while proving nothing. **The 2/120 does NOT contradict the 78 % the earlier two-device drill measured**: that drill built the state deliberately, this generator reaches it by chance — a difference in what is sampled, which is exactly why the assertion is a floor and not an equality. `unexplained()` subtracts only two documented rules (the uid sits in the target's trash, or the lot's parent row does) and is presence-only, so a row that arrives with wrong CONTENT is caught by the other five properties rather than by it.

**Not simulated, and stated on the file header rather than left to be assumed**: photos / `_imageData`, pipe / accessory / wishlist mutations, the REPLACE path (still the only auto-applying import branch, and it wipes the cellar), the cloud transport, and a wall-clock lot-id collision between two devices. 5.5 s standalone.

## The cloud transport, which was the least-guarded file in the repo

**`useGdriveSync.ts` is 2 800 lines and a mutation pass over it produced TWO mutations — the thinnest coverage of any file here**, and it happens to be the one that decides whether a backup reaches the cloud, which auto file gets overwritten, and whether a second device is told its cellar is stale. 31 cases now cover it and the two hooks beside it (`useTastingSession`, `catalogueCloudStream`). Four were probed at the code, each reddening EXACTLY ONE case with every sibling green — which is the shape that says the rule was genuinely unguarded rather than incidentally covered:

| probe | what it breaks | result |
|---|---|---|
| reverse the catalogue comparator | fetching the OLDEST catalogue back | 1 of 25 red |
| `if (ok \|\| auto)` → `if (ok)` | a failed auto-end leaves a zombie tasting that blocks every update, for ever | 1 of 80 red |

**The fourth is the instructive one and it is this repo's oldest failure shape.** `chooseAutoSaveTarget` is a PURE function with its own suite in `gdriveApi.test.ts`, so the rule looked covered — my probe did not touch it. It removed the CALL, and nothing noticed: *the wiring is what rots*, exactly as recorded at `pipeSessionsSinceMaint`, at `reDeductRestoredSessions`, at `findParityGaps`, and at `assertLotInvariants`. A tested helper plus an untested call site is one guarantee, not two.

**On salvaging an agent's work: RUN it, then probe it, and trust neither the prose nor a green dashboard.** These 700 lines came from an agent that stalled before committing, in a worktree. Running them first produced two failures — both in `.claude/worktrees/**`, where vitest's default include had collected the OTHER agents' copies of the same file names, the documented glob hazard rather than a defect in the work. With the exclusions, 249 green. **A test whose mutant cannot be reproduced is worth nothing**, which is why four probes were run rather than the header comments taken at their word; every one held, so the set was kept whole. Note the probe protocol that matters: the backup copy is taken **immediately before** applying the probe, never at the start of a session — a copy taken earlier restores away work done in between (that mistake cost a recovery once already), and `git checkout` is never the way to undo a probe on uncommitted work.

## The gate that was missing: a list renders O(1) rows

**Nothing in this repo measured the DOM.** `size:check` bounds the bundle, `theme:contrast` bounds contrast, `i18n:layout` bounds overflow, and Lighthouse runs against an EMPTY cellar — so three screens sat **thirteen seconds** from any user with all seven gates green. The product answer that suggests itself (forbid a flat list above N rows) was **considered and rejected**: it takes away a control the user chose — flat mode is how you sort by date across months — and a threshold produces a toggle that works at 499 rows and not at 501, which is a control lying about what it does.

**The defect was never the flat list. It was that nothing looked.** CLAUDE.md already carries the lesson twice — *the seed is a screen GATE, and a green run over an empty state is the most reassuring way to miss a screen*. This is the same thing one size up: **a green run over a SMALL cellar is the most reassuring way to miss a freeze.** The two browser checks share a 40-tobacco seed, so neither could ever have seen it.

**`listDomBudget.test.tsx` is that gate, and it is jsdom rather than a third opt-in browser check.** jsdom does not lay out, but it DOES build the DOM, which is the thing that regressed — and it runs on every commit, where an opt-in check goes unrun (`prune` was red for nine releases while this file asserted a clean baseline). The cost is asymmetric in the right direction: with the cap holding, the journal case renders ~60 rows in 0.8 s; with it broken it renders thousands, takes 5.7 s AND fails.

**What is asserted is the SHAPE, not a number.** Each view is rendered twice, at a small N and a large one, and the DOM must not grow with N. A node BUDGET would be a magic number to re-tune every time a card gains a chip; *doubling the data must not double the DOM* is the property itself and cannot be satisfied by tuning. Three things keep it honest: the small render must actually contain rows (a view rendering nothing would satisfy the growth assertion perfectly); a deliberately unbounded list is rendered as a CONTROL, so a `CHROME_SLACK` widened past usefulness is caught; and **both fixture sizes must sit above `PROGRESSIVE_STEP`, which is asserted rather than left to a comment** — the first version had SMALL below the cap, so the small render showed 40 rows and the large one the full 60, and every case failed on a 743-node gap that was the guard WORKING.

Probed by un-wiring the journal and the catalogue: 3 782 → 44 482 nodes and 1 078 → 12 078, each failure naming the view, both figures, and the remedy. **IT COVERED ONE DOOR AND THE SECOND ONE WAS STILL OPEN — the same shape as the note it was written beside.** This file measured only the FLAT branch, on the strength of a sentence saying the grouped branch was « naturally bounded (one collapsed row per brand) » — measured on a NINE-group fixture, so it measured nine headers. With one group EXPANDED: journal 86 → **185 093**, inventory 92 → **140 093**, catalogue 128 → **200 073**; and the catalogue with 20 000 distinct brands, everything collapsed, **140 065**. Five more cases now render each grouped list with a single group holding everything, plus two for the catalogue's headers. Probed by un-capping each in turn: each reddens exactly its own case. **The lesson is about the GATE rather than the code: a budget that measures one branch of a two-branch view certifies half a screen, and reads as certifying the screen.** **A LAST FIXED SLEEP AS AN ARRIVAL PROOF, replaced by a condition.** Every screen proves it arrived by finding a marker, and that proof used to be taken immediately after a FIXED wait — fine for a React-only screen, a timing assumption for `help`, which fetches a ~74 KB document at runtime and then source-slices it per section behind a 1500 ms sleep. Observed once as `steel/light/help: could not be reached` in ONE palette of a six-palette run: a screen reported unreachable for the machine's load rather than for anything about the app, which reads as a navigation defect. `awaitMarker` (shared, bounded) waits for the marker to attach before counting; a marker that never appears costs its budget and then fails exactly as before, so nothing is weakened. **When you add a list view, add it to `VIEWS` there** — the alternative was an ESLint rule, and it was rejected because "an unbounded collection" has no syntactic marker (`no-unscoped-lot-read` works only because it keys on a literal `.lots`), so it would have to guess that a `.map()` renders cards, which is the over-strict trap that gets correct code rewritten.

## Numbers the app printed but never computed

**Seven numeric defects, and what unites them is that each one is a figure the app DISPLAYS or STORES without ever having done the arithmetic the display implies.** Every one was reproduced before being changed.

**(1) A SUM OF TWO GRID VALUES IS NOT A GRID VALUE.** `useSessionStore`'s EDIT path caps the new weight at `avail = safeW(capL.weightG) + ow`, and `0.1 + 2.7` is `2.8000000000000003` in IEEE-754. The result was stored with a bare `String(nw)`, so the journal rendered `2,8000000000000003g` and the CSV export wrote it verbatim. Brute force says **21.2 %** of realistic pairs produce a noisy `avail`. Every other weight in that store already went through `roundWeightToUnit`; the cap was the one arithmetic result that did not. **`_persistSession`'s cap is untouched and must stay so** — `Math.min(w, safeW(lot.weightG))` has no ADDITION, so it can only return a value already on the grid, and a case pins that distinction.

**(2) `Math.round` MEANS 1 g IN GRAM MODE AND 28.35 g IN OUNCE MODE.** A weight is stored in whatever unit the user typed, so `computeYearConsumption`'s `Math.round` reported **0** for a year of three bowls at 0.09 oz and **1 oz** for 20 g actually smoked. `roundAggregateWeight` (lotUtils) is the DISPLAY grid — whole grams, 1 dp ounces — and is deliberately DISTINCT from `roundWeightToUnit`'s DEDUCTION grid: a stored balance needs more precision than a headline total. `trendPct` stays on the RAW sums, because it is a ratio and rounding its inputs would make the percentage disagree with the two numbers printed beside it. **One site was measured and deliberately NOT changed**: `HomeViewV2`'s stock tile, whose ounce branch is already FINER than its gram sibling, so the defect is backwards there — a named case says so, or the next sweep "completes" it.

**(3) THE CSV READER SAW ONE COMMA AND NOTHING ELSE.** `String(v).trim().replace(",", ".")` then `parseFloat` — so `1 234,5`, which is what a French or German spreadsheet emits, imported as **1**, and `1,234.56` as **1.234**: three orders of magnitude of stock, on a module whose stated contract is that an export edited in a spreadsheet round-trips, with the row neither `skipped` nor listed in `issues`. `readNum` now resolves both marks (the LAST one is the decimal) and strips the group separators a spreadsheet really emits — ASCII space, NBSP, narrow NBSP, thin space and the Swiss apostrophe. **A SINGLE comma stays a DECIMAL mark**, deliberately: that is what this parser has always done and what a French spreadsheet writes, so `2,000` reads as 2 and not 2000, and changing it would silently multiply every fr-locale weight by a thousand. Several commas can only be grouping.

**THAT FIX SHIPPED OVER-STRICT AND THE CORRECTION IS THE ENTRY.** The new reader refused anything `parseFloat` would have truncated — right for `1 234,5` → 1, where the answer is silently wrong by three orders of magnitude, and **WRONG for `50g`, where `parseFloat` returns 50, i.e. the correct answer.** Refusing that cell fixes no defect and destroys a weight this parser had always read; and a tin is sold as "50g" or "2oz", so a hand-built file is likely to carry one. The rule that separates the two: **a numeric prefix followed by a run holding NO DIGIT is a unit naming the column the value is already in, so the number is unambiguous.** A leading word (`env. 12` — an approximation) or a second number (`12abc34`) stays refused AND reported, which is the half that was genuinely missing. This module's contract is tolerance everywhere else — accent-insensitive headers, FR+EN aliases, `dd.mm.yyyy`, EN-locale export dates — so a number reader stricter than the rest of the parser would be the odd one out. **The generalisable half: strictness is justified by the WRONGNESS a lenient reader produces, not by leniency itself.** Two pre-existing cases used `50g` as their unreadable fixture and each records the reversal on its own assertion.

**(4) THE SILENCE WAS THE COSTLY HALF, AGAIN.** `skipped` had been computed and read by nobody once before, one importer over; here a truncated `1 234,5` looked exactly like a clean import. The result now carries an EXACT `badNumber` beside the capped detail list, a `"number"` issue kind, and `csv_issues_num` in all six dictionaries, rendered in the panel that already exists.

**(5-6) A DOT DECIMAL IN A COMMA UI.** `hBars` / `donutChart` printed `12.50` on a screen that says `12,50`, and so did the collection report — a document the user prints and files. The two chart helpers take a FORMATTER (their caller already owns their locale-dependent text) and `collectionReport.ts` takes a `formatNumber` OPTION rather than an i18n import, because its language-neutrality is its design premise; `useExportImport` gained `lang` for it, threaded from `App.tsx`. **The wiring is what rots, so it has its own test**: the module's own option-level cases stayed green when the hook was unwired, and only the three new hook cases caught it. Chart series (`catW` / `brandW`) are rounded at the source, so the caption and the bar cannot disagree.

**(7) TWO READERS OF ONE FIELD DISAGREEING.** `collectionReport.num()` used `parseFloat` where `computeStats` uses `Number`, and had no guard against a value large enough to render in exponential notation (`1e+21 €`). It coerces the way `computeStats` does now — deliberately WITHOUT comma-normalising first, because the defect is the disagreement, so the aim is the same answer as everything else, not a better one in one place.

## A test that wrote the repo it was testing

**`dataOnlyRelease.test.ts` snapshotted the real `src/constants.ts` and `public/version.json`, let the script bump them, and restored in `afterEach`.** That holds only while the process survives to the restore AND is the only writer. Neither held. **MEASURED, on a real working tree with uncommitted work in it: `src/constants.ts` came back TRUNCATED and `public/version.json` EMPTY**, and the failure surfaced as *"could not parse APP_BUILD"* — an error about the repo, not about the script, which is what made it take a while to read.

**The mechanism, because it will recur wherever git worktrees are used:** the test resolves `"src/constants.ts"` against `process.cwd()`, which is the PROJECT ROOT for every vitest worker, while the script resolves its own paths against `__dirname`. Vitest's default include is not scoped to `src/`, so three sibling worktrees under `.claude/worktrees/` were collected as extra test files, and their copies of this test read and wrote the MAIN tree's files while their copies of the script wrote their own. Four interleaved writers, one file.

**The fix is the repo's own idiom — injection over ambient.** `applyCataloguePlan` takes `nowIso`, `catalogueSave` takes `nowMs`, `useUserCatalogue` is handed `dlFile`; `bumpDataOnly` now takes an optional **`root`**, defaulting to the repo the script lives in so the CLI and `npm run release:data` are byte-identical in behaviour. The test builds a temp root and asserts **the real files are unchanged**, checked BEFORE the `afterEach` can paper over a write. Probed in both directions: ignoring `root` reddens the isolation case, and moving the DEFAULT reddens six — which is the half that matters, since a `root` that silently became mandatory would make the release write nothing where the release has to happen, and no other case would notice because they all restore what they clobber.

**Two rules for anyone running the suite here.** A test that writes the tree is a hazard whatever its cleanup; prefer a root you can point elsewhere. And **while a worktree exists under `.claude/`, run vitest with `--exclude '.claude/**'`** — otherwise its test files are collected as extra copies with the wrong cwd. The same goes for `eslint .`, which reports a parse error per worktree file (multiple candidate `tsconfigRootDir`s); lint the real paths instead.

## Costs paid on every save

**Three measured costs, and the third is a correction to the finding rather than a fix for it.**

**(1) EVERY COVER PHOTO WAS READ FROM INDEXEDDB TWICE, ON EVERY SAVE.** MEASURED by driving the real `App` over a 300-cover cellar with a counting `imgCache`: **600 `imgCache.get` calls on the cold load and 600 more per save** — a save that touched only `nxT`. **TWO overlapping effects** walked the same collections (one filtered to `local-photo-*` and also covering the session snapshots, one taking every `imageUrl` and covering none), and both wrote back through `imgMap(prev, upd)` — a FRESH object — so `imgLocal` changed identity on every save even when the resolved set was byte-identical, re-rendering every consumer of the map for nothing. The second effect is DELETED (it could only ever contribute a key that is truthy, resolvable from `imgCache` and NOT `local-photo-*`, and `migrateData._clearForeignImageRefs` blanks every `imageUrl` that is not a `local-photo-*` key or a `data:image/…` URI, while a `data:` URI is never an `imgCache` key — pinned by a case, so the deletion cannot quietly start losing photos), and the survivor skips a key the map already holds. **The skip is ONE-DIRECTIONAL and that is the design**: a key NOT in the map is still asked for on every pass, because a miss means the blob is not there YET (an eviction, or an import whose `_imageData` writes land after its `save`), so remembering a miss would make that photo unrecoverable for the session. After: **300 reads on cold load, 0 per save**, and the pre-existing `if (Object.keys(upd).length)` guard now preserves the map's identity. `imgLocalRef` is a latest-ref MIRROR written from an EFFECT, never at render time (`react-hooks/refs` is error-level here) — and it is not one render stale, because React runs a commit's effects in DECLARATION order and the mirror is declared far above the resolver.

**(2) THE CATALOGUE FILTERED EVERY ROW ON EVERY KEYSTROKE.** MEASURED: 6-13 ms per key at 1594 rows, **66-148 ms at 20 000** (`MAX_CATALOGUE_ROWS`), the cost rising with query length because a longer token falls through to the Levenshtein fallback; a phone is several times slower again. Typing "capstan blue" paid that twelve times, each pass blocking the keystroke after it. `CATALOG_SEARCH_DEBOUNCE_MS` is 160 — above a fast typist's 120-200 ms inter-key gap, below the ~200 ms at which a delayed UI reads as lag. **The FIELD is never debounced** (the prefill-race rule — debouncing the input itself makes it snap back under the user's fingers); only the query the filter reads. **An EMPTY query applies at once**, so clearing never leaves a stale list, and the `catalogSeed` deep-link applies at once too, since that page's contract is that a search hit opens it PRE-FILTERED.

**(3) `appCtx` — MEASURED, AND THE REPORTED FIX WOULD NOT HAVE HELPED.** The finding was that `appCtx` is a fresh object literal every render, so one save re-runs every mounted view's memos (150-172 ms). Driving the real `App` over a 300-tobacco / 200-pipe / 3000-session cellar, a render that changed **no data** (flipping the Settings tab) cost **63.1 / 53.4 ms — and no view memo re-ran.** `useMemo` compares deps BY VALUE, so a new context identity re-renders consumers without invalidating their memos; and on a SAVE the data genuinely changes, so a memo over `appCtx` would not have stopped that cascade either. The whole cost was two plain **render-body expressions** in `HomeViewV2` (`ghostExclude` / `accordPrefer`) calling `computePipeGhostingRisk` and `pipeAccordsWithFamily` once per pipe — 200 + 200 calls, ~1.3 M session rows — which sit BELOW the view's early return, where a `useMemo` would break hook order, and which are paid only while the Home is on screen (a correction to the "from every screen" framing). The lever is the one `ghosting.ts` already documents: both helpers now take the optional precomputed `PipeCategoryIndex`, built once by the Home. After: **36.5 / 27.0 ms** on a non-data render, **69.1 ms** on a save (was 94.2). **`appCtx` is deliberately left UN-MEMOISED** — a hand-written dep list over **394 keys** (MEASURED — this said « ~200 », roughly half the real figure, and the rule about quoting a number you produced applies to this file's own) is the stale-closure class several builds were spent removing, and it would not have removed this cost. **The lesson is the shape: a finding can name a real symptom, a real cause and the wrong remedy, and only the measurement separates them.**

**FOUR consumers now take that index** (`pipeSessionsSinceMaint`, `computePipeUsageProfile`, `computePipeGhostingRisk`, `pipeAccordsWithFamily`) — if a fifth appears, hoist one index for all of them rather than adding a fifth optional parameter.

## Two writes in one handler, and four other ways a callback wrote too late

**THE MECHANISM, not the instance.** Both stores build their payload from the same render's `data`, and `save` is a `useCallback([])` doing `setData(nd)` — React does not re-render between two calls in one synchronous handler. So a handler that chains TWO store mutations has its FIRST write overwritten, in React state **and on disk**, since `appStorage.set` writes synchronously. Reproduced on `SessionFormView.submit`, the only such handler in the app: the session landed, the lot was debited, and the `tastingNotes` write vanished. Fixed at the mechanism because a per-call-site fix leaves every future two-mutation handler broken: `App.tsx` keeps a **`latestDataRef`**, mirrored at all four `setData` sites (including inside `save`), exposed as a stable `latestData()`; the two stores shadow `data` with it at the top of each mutation. It is only ever AHEAD of `data`, never behind, and it is OPTIONAL so an unwired store degrades silently to the old behaviour — **which is exactly why the wiring is asserted at source level.**

**The legacy inline-base64 photo migration was rebased onto it**, like the trash sweep and the orphan GC: it captured `data`, awaited the IndexedDB writes, then saved from the CAPTURED snapshot, reverting anything done in that window. **A naive rebase is NOT enough, and this is the part worth knowing:** the old swap matched its task by OBJECT IDENTITY (`x.item === item`), and a concurrent save rebuilds every row — so against the fresh cellar nothing would match and the swap would become a silent no-op, with photos written to IndexedDB, never referenced, and the base64 left inline for ever. The pure `applyMigratedPhotoKeys` matches on `(collection, id, dataURL)`; the dataURL is part of the key so a photo the user replaced mid-write is left alone, and it returns the SAME object when nothing applies so the migration cannot dirty the cellar on every launch.

**Three more, each a dead end rather than a race.** **(a) The export could run twice**: the JSZip loader appended a second `<script>` on a second tap, and `_runZip` / `doExport` had no busy latch — one shared in-flight promise and one latch each. **(b) The factory reset raced its own cloud backup**: `save(Object.assign({}, INIT))` arms the 1.2 s debounced auto-save, and the `await imgCache.clear()` after it can outlast that on a photo-heavy cellar, so `gdriveSaveQuiet` uploaded the EMPTY cellar and stamped `cave-autosave-ts`. Fixed by ORDER — auto-save is disarmed before the cellar is blanked, so the debounce has nothing to fire against. **(c) "Restaurer" then "Annuler" muted a genuinely-newer backup for ever**: `_executeCloudNewerRestore` acked the moment the download parsed, writing the persistent dismissed-name marker before the picker had even opened, and `cancelImport` only clears the picker. `stageImport` gained **`onApplied`**, fired after `save(next)` on BOTH modes (a merge puts the file's contents in the cellar too) and try/catch-guarded so a throwing caller cannot take a committed import down with it. **Ack-on-applied was chosen over re-arm-on-cancel deliberately**: re-arming would have to restore previous marker values nothing captured, and would leave a window in which the app has already forgotten a warning it may still need.

**And one clock guard.** `isWithinDays` had no UPPER bound, so `Date.now() - future` is negative and a future timestamp read as "still within" **permanently** — silently disarming the 7-day quota-warning dismissal and the 30-day backup reminder, both of which use it to SUPPRESS. `EB.getDerivedStateFromError` already carried the identical guard, which is what identifies the shape.

## The right data written to the wrong place — five doors, one shape

Something asynchronous finishes and writes into whatever is current NOW rather than into what asked for it. None of the five reports anything: the user sees a fiche they did not edit change under them, or a field they just typed revert.

**(1) A FINISHED AI ANSWER LANDED ON WHATEVER FICHE WAS OPEN.** The provider call has a 60 s abort budget, is not cancelled on navigation, and carried no identity — so: open tobacco A, tap « Rechercher », back out through the unsaved-changes guard, open tobacco B, and A's brand, name, category, blend, force, room note, taste, ageing and description were merged into B. ## Four values that made the app never open again

**The class, before the instances.** Each of these arrives through the same ordinary door — a JSON import or a cloud restore of a file you did not write — and each is written to disk by `save()` BEFORE anything renders, so the next launch fails identically. **Neither recovery path helps**: `EB.purgeCachesAndReload` and `public/reset.html` both clear service workers and Cache Storage and deliberately touch neither `localStorage` nor IndexedDB. The only escape was clearing site data or deleting the PWA, which loses the cellar. That asymmetry — a boundary that can recover a bad CHUNK but not a bad VALUE — is why this class is worth its own section.

**(1) A BROWSER THAT REFUSES SITE STORAGE GAVE A BLANK PAGE, AND NOT EVEN THE REPAIR LINK.** Safari (macOS and iOS) with « Block all cookies », Chrome or Firefox with site data blocked for the origin, and some MDM profiles do not hand back an empty store: touching `localStorage` THROWS `SecurityError`. **No file, no import, no hand-editing — a first visit is enough.** `main.jsx` guards every one of its pre-mount reads and its catch says why; `App` did not, so the first render threw. That alone is survivable — the boundary exists for it — except that **`EB.render()` read `cave-lang` from the same store**, in the branch that only runs once something has already crashed. So the boundary threw the identical error while rendering the screen meant to explain it, React unmounted the whole tree, and `#root` was left EMPTY — and the boot shell in `index.html`, **including the « Rien ne se passe ? Réparer l'application → » link**, lives inside `#root` and had already been replaced. A white page with nothing on it. **THE RULE: the error boundary may not depend on anything that can be the thing that failed.** Every storage read on the render path now goes through the crash-safe `lsGet`, swept over `src/` and locked by `storageBlockedBoot.test.ts`. **The exemptions are stated on the test and are not oversights**: `appStorage.ts` IS the helper; `main.jsx`'s reads are each individually try-wrapped and run before React exists; and the **OAuth / token domain keeps its raw reads** because `no-storage-read-after-remove` matches a LITERAL `getItem` against a literal `removeItem` — route those through a helper and that rule goes blind to the read-before-clear invariant, which is the more valuable guarantee of the two. A case asserts each exempt file still reads storage, so an exemption cannot become dead licence.

**(2) AN OBJECT IN `name` OR `brand` BRICKED EVERY LAUNCH.** `_coerceStringFields` normalised `number` and `boolean` and let everything else fall through, so `"name": {"fr":"N"}` reached React as a child — *Objects are not valid as a React child* — and the Home mounts on every launch. Now TOTAL. **An ARRAY joins and an OBJECT blanks**, which is a decision rather than a detail: `["Halvorsen"]` is a shape a hand-edited or machine-generated file really produces and the word inside it is the user's own data, so blanking would throw away something recoverable; `String({})` is `"[object Object]"`, developer jargon leaking onto a fiche, with nothing to recover. A sweep of every documented string field × ten hostile values through the real migration and the real views found that **only `name` and `brand` on a tobacco or a pipe brick the APP** — the rest degrade one fiche.

**(3) `rating: 1e999` LOCKED THE MAIN THREAD FOR EVER.** `JSON.parse` turns that into `Infinity`, nothing clamped it, and `Stars`' sequenced entry animation registers one timer per star in a `for (i = 1; i <= n; i++)` loop. Opening that fiche hung the tab outright — it had to be force-killed, and the value is persisted, so every later attempt did it again; the user's only escape was deleting the row from the LIST without ever tapping it, which nothing tells them. **The evidence is the reproduction: the test file OOM-killed the vitest worker before the fix.** Fixed in BOTH places on purpose — `migrateData` clamps the stored value, and `Stars` clamps its PROP, because `n` can reach the component by routes the migration never sees (a computed average, a future field) and only five stars are ever drawn, so more than five timers is waste on any input.

**(4) A FORGED `ai-provider` THREW ON EVERY RENDER — and the systemic half matters more than the instance.** `AI_MODEL_OPTIONS[provider]` on a plain object resolves `__proto__` to `Object.prototype`, which is TRUTHY, so the `|| []` fallback never fires and `.filter is not a function`. Lowest reachability of the four (hand-edited storage only — the `_settings` import path validates this key against a closed set). **The ESLint rule written for exactly this class could not see it: `no-dynamic-index-plain-map` bailed on any declaration whose kind was not `const`, and this project declares its lookup tables as `export var` throughout.** Measured: `npx eslint src/hooks/useAiAutoFill.ts` reported ZERO on a live defect. **`var` is now in scope; `let` stays exempt**, which is the distinction the original reasoning was really about.

**WIDENING THAT RULE IMMEDIATELY MET THE OVER-STRICT TRAP, and how it was resolved is the durable part.** It reported SEVEN sites — and **five were already correct**, each carrying an inline `Object.prototype.hasOwnProperty.call(MAP, k)` and a comment explaining it (`FAMILY_AGING_MAX`, `CUT_DENSITY`, `HEADER_ALIASES`, `LANG_ASSETS`, and one more). Flagging them left two bad options: revert the widening and lose the live defect it had just found, or bolt an `eslint-disable` onto five correct sites, **which teaches the next reader to silence the rule**. So the rule learned to recognise the guard: an own-property test on the SAME map, in the SAME statement (`Object.hasOwn` accepted as the modern spelling). **The scoping is deliberate and both halves are pinned as invalid fixtures** — a guard on a DIFFERENT map in the same statement does not launder the read, and a guard several statements away is a control-flow claim this rule cannot verify. **What is NOT accepted is a call to a named predicate that wraps the test** (`isLangLoaded(code)`): following that means resolving an arbitrary function, and the honest answer for such a map is to build it null-prototype — which is what the two genuinely-unguarded sites did (`LANG`, whose own comment used to say *"the rule does not fire here because LANG is a var"*, and `_LANG_COL`).

## The local day — and the five sites that used the UTC one

**`parseLocalDate` has existed for exactly this, its own comment spells the mechanism out, and `daysSince` — the ONE helper feeding every lot age in the app — never went through it.** `new Date("2026-08-24")` is UTC midnight; diffed against a local `Date.now()` it shifts the answer by a day for part of every day. **MEASURED on the real function at every local hour:** Los Angeles wrong from **17:00 to 23:59** (ages one day too HIGH), Auckland wrong **all morning**, and **Paris wrong from midnight to 02:00** (too LOW). So this was never an exotic-timezone corner: the author's own zone is affected every night, and the western window is the EVENING — precisely when somebody smokes a bowl and logs it. `pipeRestDays`, `computePipeRest`, `suggest.ts` and `HomeViewV2` all used the local parser already; this was the survivor. What it drives: `fmtLotAge` on the lot row, the card and the fiche; « ouvert depuis N »; `isRecentPurchase` (so a lot left the « Achats récents » chip a day early); `lotMaturityBucket`'s young→optimal split; `scopedOldestAgeDays`; the CSV export's Age column; « nettoyée il y a N jours » on the pipe fiche.

**`localDayKey(ms)` is now THE local-day formatter and `today()` is `localDayKey(Date.now())`.** Four display/write sites still built a day from `toISOString().slice(0,10)` — the UTC day — surviving the sweep that created `today()`: the **CSV importer's lifecycle back-fill**, which WRITES into the cellar (a western evening import produced a lot « mise en pot » TOMORROW, and nothing warns because `daysSince` clamps a negative age to 0); the **collection report's « Généré le »**, so the document a user files with an insurer was stamped tomorrow and disagreed with the name of the file it arrived in; and two Settings lines (« Catalogue chargé le », the stale-version-check date). `localDayBoundary.test.ts` sweeps `src/` for the CONSTRUCT rather than for any one spelling, **with comments blanked first** — both fixed sites explain themselves by naming what they replaced, and a check that reads its own prose as data is the trap this repo has now been caught by four times.

**THE ACTIVITY STRIP WALKED FIXED 86 400 000 ms STEPS, IN THREE COPIES.** A local day is 23 or 25 hours long twice a year, so the walk SKIPPED a calendar day in spring and emitted one TWICE in autumn — reproduced in Paris and Los Angeles, in both directions. The consequence is not cosmetic: the total above the strip counted a session whose cell was never drawn (measured: `total` 3, cells summing to 2), and tapping the affected cell filtered the journal by the wrong date. `Charts.jsx`'s Stats calendar already walked it with `setDate`; the Home strip did not. **The geometry had THREE hand-rolled copies** — the grid builder, the month ticks, and `HomeViewV2`'s own tap handler — so the fix is one exported `heatmapDayKeys(weeks, nowMs)` every consumer reads, walked with `setDate` at NOON (so the ±1 h a transition moves the wall clock cannot push a date across a midnight of its own).

**A pre-existing test PINNED one of these defects**: `fmtLotAge(364)` asserted `"12age_mo"` — twelve months is a year — two lines above a case named *« caps months at 11 »* that covered the YEAR branch only. The suite asserted a rule and its violation side by side; the reversal is recorded on the assertion.

**THE TEST FORCES A TIMEZONE, and that is the load-bearing part.** The container runs in UTC, where every one of these assertions passes on the OLD code — a test taking the runner's zone would have been vacuous, which is how the defect survived a suite this size. Node re-reads `process.env.TZ` per `Date` call since v16, so setting it in a hook is enough; UTC is kept as the CONTROL, since it is the one zone the old code was already right in and a fix that broke it would show up there.

**DISCLOSED AND NOT FIXED — a clock set forward by more than 30 days empties the trash, unattended.** `sweepExpiredTrash` runs 6 s after launch against `Date.now() - 30 days`, so a device whose clock is a year fast hard-deletes every trashed row and strips `lotId` from their sessions, with no confirmation and no message. Reachability is genuinely low (a dead-battery boot before NTP resync, an emulator, a hand-set date). **The obvious guard is WRONG and that is why this is written down rather than half-fixed**: refusing to sweep when the cut-off is newer than every stamp in the cellar would break the ordinary case of a user who simply has not opened the app in two months — their stamps are all old and the sweep is exactly what should happen. Do not ship that guard without a way to tell the two apart.

**FERMÉ — EN CESSANT DE CHERCHER À LES DISTINGUER.** La note ci-dessus a raison sur le fond et pose une exigence impossible : **aucune donnée locale ne sépare les deux cas**, puisque tous deux mettent `Date.now()` loin devant chaque tampon de la cave. Les seules issues seraient une horloge de confiance (l'en-tête `Date` d'une réponse serveur — donc un réseau que ce chemin, différé de 6 s au lancement, n'a pas) ou une horloge monotone inter-sessions, que le navigateur n'offre pas. Le correctif renonce donc au discriminateur et **borne le rayon** : `trustedSweepNow` (utils.ts) plafonne l'avance de l'horloge du balayage à `TRASH_RETENTION_DAYS` par lancement, contre une marque persistée (`cave-sweep-clock`).

**LA GARANTIE, dans l'ordre où elle compte** : une ligne supprimée DEPUIS le lancement précédent ne peut pas être purgée par un saut d'horloge, parce que la coupure ne peut pas dépasser la marque précédente — c'est ce qui fixe le plafond à 30 jours et pas plus. Qui ouvre l'app au moins une fois par mois n'est jamais bridé, à l'octet près. Une absence réelle de six mois converge en six lancements, et le seul coût entre-temps est une corbeille qui se vide plus tard. Une horloge qui RECULE — la correction NTP après un démarrage aberrant — est absorbée par le `min` : la marque suit vers le bas, donc une mauvaise lecture ne reste pas gravée.

**CE QUE ÇA N'EST PAS, énoncé plutôt que laissé à découvrir** : ce n'est pas une détection d'horloge fausse. Le rayon est borné, pas nul — une ligne qui existait DÉJÀ au lancement précédent peut encore partir en avance. Et la première exécution (marque absente) fait confiance à `Date.now()`, faute de base pour borner : restaurer une sauvegarde sur une machine déjà mal réglée tombe dans ce cas, exactement comme avant. **Le plafond ne descend pas en dessous de 30 jours** : plus bas, quelqu'un qui ouvre l'app toutes les six semaines dériverait indéfiniment et la corbeille afficherait des expirations qu'elle ne tient pas.

**Un cas pré-existant a rougi et il avait raison** : il épinglait `Date.now() - TRASH_RETENTION_DAYS` sur la ligne de coupure. Ce qu'il protège est le SIGNE (un `+` purge toute la corbeille 30 jours trop tôt), pas la provenance de l'horloge ; repointé sur `- TRASH_RETENTION_DAYS`, avec le renversement consigné sur l'assertion. Sondé trois fois — revenir à `Date.now()` nu, ne plus réécrire la marque, retirer le `min` — chacune rougit sa cible et rien d'autre.

## Mesurer plutôt que redouter : dix mutations, dix tuées

**La note voisine dit « 15 des 21 hooks et l'essentiel des ~29 vues NON MESURÉS », et c'était vrai au sens littéral — personne n'avait mesuré. Mesuré maintenant, le risque qu'elle laissait craindre ne s'est pas matérialisé.** Dix règles choisies par CONSÉQUENCE (une rupture qui perd des données ou ment en silence), chacune injectée puis confrontée à la suite ENTIÈRE, la seule question étant « est-ce que quelque chose, n'importe où, l'attrape » : `wishToInv` perdant `catalogueLock` (le verrou par fiche que la passe catalogue en masse doit respecter) · la garde de quota ne regardant plus que le quota d'origine, c'est-à-dire le mauvais budget · un accessoire supprimé DUR, sans corbeille ni annulation · `deleteSession` sans sa garde d'idempotence (grammes crédités deux fois) · `useProgressiveList` remettant son compteur à zéro sur changement d'identité (bouton qui ne révèle plus rien) · `useDbSync` admettant `tastingNotes` dans le diff, le seul champ qui est la prose de l'utilisateur · `isDirty` toujours faux (on quitte un formulaire modifié sans un mot) · `expiresAt` ignoré (annonce périmée pour toujours) · la fiche tabac cessant de suivre la portée du filtre · `canIgnite` n'exigeant plus un lot résoluble — le défaut exact qui a détruit une dégustation entière.

**DIX SUR DIX TUÉES.** Recensement joint : 31 vues sur 33 ont leur propre fichier, et les deux autres sont couvertes par `untestedModules.test.tsx`.

**CE QUE ÇA NE DIT PAS, et c'est la moitié qui compte** : dix règles ne sont pas une couverture. Elles ont été choisies parce qu'elles sont les plus coûteuses à rompre, donc aussi les plus susceptibles d'avoir été gardées. Ce qui reste non mesuré est la QUEUE — les chemins secondaires de ces mêmes hooks, et les règles que je n'ai pas choisies. **La leçon est sur la note plutôt que sur le code : « non mesuré » et « non gardé » ne sont pas la même chose, et confondre les deux fabrique une dette qui n'existe pas.** Une note qui déclare une zone non mesurée doit être refermée par une MESURE, pas par une intuition — dans un sens comme dans l'autre.

## Lot identity across devices — the two silent duplications

**Two ways a LOT was duplicated silently, and the missing net that made both invisible.** Both found by the pre-public-release integrity drill, both reproduced before anything was changed. They share a shape: the same physical tin ends up counted twice, the per-device numeric `id` differs so no id rule fires, and the error is an UNDERFLOW while `lot-balance-overflow` is deliberately one-sided — across ~1500 drilled end-states that rule fired **zero** times.

**HIGH-1 — merging while a lot sits in this device's trash appended the imported twin.** The lot dedup seen-sets (`useImportConfirm`) were built from LIVE lots only, so an imported lot whose twin was in the trash matched nothing. Measured at **16 % of 400 randomised two-device merges**, ~123 g of ghost stock each. The sequence is ordinary: delete a lot on one phone, then merge the other's backup — or tap the cloud-newer banner's « Restaurer ». **Exactly this fix was made one level up, for entities, and lots never got it**; the fix mirrors it — SKIP rather than resurrect (the trashed lot IS this tin, so a copy duplicates it, while un-deleting overrules a deliberate deletion), and COUNT it (`lotsTrashedSkipped` + `merge_recap_trashed_lots`, six languages), because silence reads as « ma sauvegarde n'a pas rendu ma boîte ». A uid-less legacy lot still matches on `lotMergeKey` only — disclosed, not guessed at.

**HIGH-2 — « Tout restaurer » resurrected the source half of a duplicate-merge move.** `mergeDuplicates` — the app's own healing tool for the doubling — moves lots onto the kept row and soft-deletes the originals, with the trash documented as the undo. Measured: 110 g / 2 lots → merge → 110 g / 2 lots → Tout restaurer → **160 g / 3 lots**, invariants silent, one button, no confirm. The same doubling was closed through the per-row door and this one left open. **The signal needs no new field**: the move carries the uid onto the kept lot, so after a move the same uid is LIVE on one lot and trashed on another, and that state has no other legitimate cause. `restoreAllFromTrash` skips those; **`restoreFromTrash` and `restoreSelectionFromTrash` are untouched on purpose** — there the user names one lot, and that choice is theirs.

**The net: `lot-uid-unique`.** The lot `uid` has been the cross-device identity and was **the one identity with no uniqueness rule at all**. Scoped GLOBALLY (a merge moves tins between rows, so per-row scope would miss it) and skipping trashed lots — which is what makes it agree with HIGH-2's rule rather than fight it. It fires on the end state both defects produced.

**Three methodological notes, and the second is the one that generalises.** (a) The new rule shipped without its `Array.isArray` guard and the PRE-EXISTING "survives a garbage payload" case caught it on the first run — the guard is not hypothetical, `data` comes straight from disk or an imported file. (b) **My HIGH-2 test passed while the fix was deleted.** It re-implemented the hook's rule inline instead of driving the hook, and its source-level companion matched `liveLotUids[l.uid]` — which is also how the map is POPULATED. Both were rewritten to drive the real `useTrashOps`; only then does the probe redden. Same lesson as before: *when a probe stays green, find out which layer absorbs it.* (c) A third case expected 160 g after a per-row restore and was WRONG — the restored lot's PARENT row is itself trashed by the merge, so it is not yet in the cellar. The assertion now says that, and says it was corrected.

**Probed in three directions, each reddening alone.** Still open from the same drill and NOT fixed here: the other device's consumption never reaches your stock and the merge reports nothing at all (78 % of merges detach at least one session — the detachment is a deliberate choice, the SILENCE is not); a primitive element in `data.pipes` makes `migrateData` throw on the unguarded auto-apply path; and CSV re-import compounds on a cellar that already holds two same-name rows (28/300, announced each time but the recap does not convey that repeating multiplies).

**THIRD way, and it is the same tin under a DIFFERENT ROW. The lot uid sets were per-tobacco while every other consumer of a lot uid is global.** The trashed-twin fix built its sets from the MATCHED tobacco's own lots, which is the scope `lotMergeKey` needs and not the scope a `uid` has: `lot-uid-unique` says so in its own comment — *"scoped GLOBALLY, not per tobacco, because the tins it protects can end up under different rows (a merge moves them)"* — and `restoreAllFromTrash` walks every tobacco for exactly that reason. **What moves a lot between rows is `mergeDuplicates`, the app's own healing tool for the doubling**, which carries the `uid` onto the kept row and soft-deletes the source copy. So: A and B both hold the pre-uid doubling (T1/T2, same `brand|name`, distinct entity uids, the tin's lot under T2); A merges T2 into T1, backs up; B merges that backup — staged T1 matches B's T1 by uid, B's T1 has no lots, the per-row seen-set is EMPTY and the tin is **APPENDED**, while staged T2's lots are all `deletedAt` and skipped and `deletedAt` is LWW-protected, so B's T2 keeps its live copy. **200 g where the user has 100.** `lot-balance-overflow` cannot see it (deliberately one-sided; this is an underflow) and the recap reported it as an ordinary `merge_recap_lots`; `lot-uid-unique` DOES fire, which is the only reason it was not silent — a post-hoc diagnostic, not a guard. **`lotMergeKey` deliberately STAYS per-tobacco**: acquisition identity (box + price + dates) means something only within one blend, and two different blends bought the same day at the same price are not the same tin — widening it would DROP a real lot, so that half is asserted separately. The global sets are kept LIVE as lots are appended, and an added-wholesale row registers its lots too. Reproduced end to end before the fix; probed by neutering the global registration (8 cases redden, including the trashed-twin ones — the global set subsumes the per-row one).


## A forged imageUrl, and why the guard sits at the choke point

**A forged backup BRICKED the app permanently, and the fix had to be at the choke point rather than the source.** Found by the pre-public-release security review, reproduced by me before being written down. A `.json` backup carrying `imageUrl: "__proto__"` survives `migrateData` (its guard-rail only blanked `^https?://` at the time — see the allowlist that replaced it), and the photo lookup all **18 render sites** share — `(imgLocal && imgLocal[x.imageUrl]) || x.imageUrl` over a PLAIN object — returns **`Object.prototype`**, a truthy non-string. `safeBgUrl` then called `url.indexOf("blob:")` on it and threw.

**What made it the worst finding rather than a crash**: `save(next)` runs BEFORE the render, so the poisoned row is already in `localStorage`; the inventory list throws unconditionally; and **neither recovery path clears localStorage** — `EB.purgeCachesAndReload` and `public/reset.html` both stop at service workers and Cache Storage (verified in both files). One imported file therefore showed the error boundary on every launch, for ever, with the user's own cellar stranded, escapable only by clearing site data or deleting the PWA. Not reachable via CSV (`parseTobaccoCsv` forces `imageUrl: ""`); JSON import and cloud restore only. **This is exactly the class the public release opens** — until now the only person who could hand this app a backup was its owner.

**The same field, the other direction, and the sink `safeBgUrl` does not cover.** The guard-rail left in `migrateData` anchored on `^https?://`, which is the LOSING side of this question: a **protocol-relative** `//evil.com/beacon.png` and a `data:image/svg+xml` both walked straight past it. All 38 background sites route through `safeBgUrl`, which correctly returns `""` for both — but the form's photo PREVIEW is **the one bare `<img src>` in the app**, fed by `imgLocal?.[form.imageUrl] || form.imageUrl`, so a lookup miss puts the raw string in `src`. Opening the edit form of an imported item therefore fired a request to the author's host: IP, User-Agent, and confirmation that the file was imported. Not code execution — an `<img>` cannot run SVG script — but a **tracking beacon inside a shared backup**, and it contradicts `public/privacy.html` verbatim (« Photos (locales uniquement) … ne transitent par aucun service tiers »). Reachable via JSON import (BOTH replace and merge — `migrateData` runs on the merge result too) and via cloud restore; NOT via CSV, which forces `imageUrl: ""`. **The fix is an ALLOWLIST, `isLocalPhotoRef`, because enumerating what is BAD is exactly what had already missed twice from one anchor**: an `imageUrl` has had precisely two legitimate shapes made the app local-photos-only — a `local-photo-*` key, or a `data:image/(jpeg|jpg|png|webp|gif);` URI, which is the **quota fallback** and must keep working (a naive `isSafeExternalUrl` guard here would have broken it, which is why the sink was left unguarded in the first place). Applied at BOTH ends sharing that one predicate — `migrateData` (renamed `_clearForeignImageRefs`, since a name that says "external URL" would now be lying) so nothing poisoned is ever stored, and `safeImgSrc` at the sink so a value arriving any other way still cannot beacon. An unresolved `local-photo-*` key is deliberately still passed to `src`: as a relative URL it 404s on our own origin, no external request, which is the pre-existing behaviour for an evicted photo. Probed three ways (restore the anchor, restore the bare `<img>`, widen the allowlist to any `data:`) — 6, 1 and 4 cases redden respectively. **A case had asserted that `migrateData` does NOT blank `"__proto__"`, with the stated reason that scrubbing it would make the downstream guards pass vacuously — that is exactly what happened, and the reversal is recorded on the assertion**: those guards are defence in depth for a caller handing over a plain map (every fixture, three form views), so they are still exercised directly, without going through the source.

**Same build — the cloud-newer banner said « synchroniser » and did a REPLACE.** `restoreCloudNewerBackup` is the app's only `autoApply: "replace"` call site: it skips the Replace/Merge picker, shows no diff and offers no undo, while all six languages' `cloud_newer_banner` said *restore it to SYNC this device* — and sync is the MERGE word. It matters most for the exact user it appears to, because `findNewerCloudBackup` compares the cloud file against **this device's last CLOUD save, not its last local edit**, so the banner surfaces precisely when there is unsynced local work to lose. The text now names the replacement in each language's own word (remplace / replaces / sustituye / ersetzt / sostituisce / substitui), and a test asserts none of them says "sync" and that there is still exactly ONE `autoApply: "replace"` call site — the wording is only honest while the action stays the one it describes. **SETTLED BY THE USER: it goes through the Replace/Merge PICKER now**, so the app has NO auto-applied replace left in production. The decision it reverses is recorded in `useImportConfirm` («the picker would just add an extra confirm step with no useful choice — there's no other reasonable interpretation»); there IS another one, MERGE, and this is exactly the banner where it matters, since `findNewerCloudBackup` compares against this device's last CLOUD save rather than its last local edit. **`setImportModal(true)` is REQUIRED alongside the staging, not decoration**: the picker is rendered by `SettingsModal`, which `CuratorApp` only mounts while `importModal` is set, so staging without opening it would leave the import pending with NOTHING on screen — a worse defect than the one being fixed. Two tests recorded the reversal on their own assertions (`useGdriveSync.test.ts`'s « the third arg requests an auto-replace, so the user never sees the picker », and the « exactly ONE call site » case, which now asserts ZERO across all three hooks with comments stripped first). The `autoApply` OPTION stays — `"merge"` has a live caller (the CSV import) sharing the same code path. Probed: restoring the auto-replace reddens 2 cases. **The size question the previous sentence left open was settled, and it is NOT "raise Restaurer to 44".** The state was: `Restaurer` at `minHeight: 44` on Home and **28 in the global Overlays copy**, with a **28 px dismiss ×** beside it in a `gap: 8` row. ## A lot says where its grams went

**A lot now says where its grams went, and the feature was the small half of the build.** Reported: « quand je vais sur un lot j'aimerais avoir la date de chaque session avec le grammage fumé avec un lien sur la session ». **Three things were EXTRACTED or REUSED rather than written a second time**, which is this file's most repeated lesson. (a) `compareSessionsRecent` (utils.ts) is JournalView's inline `sortBy === "date"` comparator, moved: two of its four rungs are not re-derivable by eye — timed-before-untimed is handled EXPLICITLY because locale collation does not rank punctuation against digits predictably, and the final `id`-DESC tie-break is what keeps the order stable after a restore scrambles `data.sessions`. (b) `sessionsForLot` owns the filter, `String()`-ed on BOTH sides because the lot `id` is numeric and `session.lotId` is a string — a raw `===` matches nothing and would render an empty list on a lot that has been smoked. (c) the row's accessible name is `aria_session_card`, the key the journal cards already use, so the two doors to a session announce identically.

**`crossOpenSession` (App.tsx) is the counterpart of `crossOpenDetail` for the one overlay that is not an entity fiche, and it needed NO change to the back engine.** It records the origin with `pushDrillOrigin` and deliberately does NOT set `drillOverlayRef`: that latch means "the open OVERLAY was drill-opened" and `decideBack`'s pop branch lists only detail/pipe/acc/wishlist, so claiming it for a session modal would assert something the branch cannot see. **THE OTHER HALF OF THE BUILD: rendering the modal found a PRE-EXISTING clip that three checkers cannot see.** In German the Close/Delete/Edit row needed **349 px in a 340 px box at the DEFAULT text size** (375 at "L") and the third button read « BEARBE », cut at the panel edge — measured identical at HEAD, so it is not from this change, and it bites an ordinary German phone at the default setting rather than at some extreme. **Probed in seven directions**, each reddening on its own: dropping either load-bearing rung of the comparator, keeping soft-deleted sessions, neutering the row handler, dropping `capHeight`, dropping the overscroll containment, passing every session unfiltered, and removing the wrap. **One probe came back green and the FIXTURE was at fault, not the code** — the timed-before-untimed case used ids whose DESC order happened to agree with the right answer, so the tie-break absorbed the mutation; the ids were inverted so the later rung now contradicts the earlier one. Same shape as the photo cap: *when a probe stays green, find out which layer absorbs it.* **A process note worth keeping: `git checkout <file>` was used to undo a probe on an UNCOMMITTED file and reverted the whole feature with it.** Recovered from a scratch copy. Undo a probe by restoring the copy you took, never with `git checkout`, while the work is unstaged.


## The session mechanic — which lots a session may charge

**The session mechanic audited end to end, and THREE of its findings were one root cause: two predicates disagreed about which lots a session may charge.** `pickJarLot` accepted any jar; `isUsableLot` refuses a jar carrying an EXPLICIT `"0"` (an empty tin). The disagreement is reachable and was **REPRODUCED before anything was changed**: a tobacco with a 0 g jar AND a full cellar lot is offered in both session pickers (the cellar lot makes it usable), and the auto-select then landed on the EMPTY JAR — which is absent from the picker's option list. **A `<select>` whose value matches no option displays its FIRST one**, so the screen showed the cellar lot while the state held the empty jar, and the session was capped to 0 g against it. From there the two visible symptoms follow: a live tasting could be ignited pointing at a lot that resolves to nothing, and 95 minutes later the auto-end **DESTROYED it** — `_persistSession` refuses a positive weight with no resolvable lot, and the auto path clears the tasting state whether the save succeeded or not (a stuck zombie tasting being the worse outcome), then announced « clôturée automatiquement après N min ». A whole dégustation lost, under a success sentence. **Four fixes, and they are deliberately layered rather than one clever guard**: `pickJarLot` now takes `isUsableLot` (still jar-only — that is its contract, and `_persistSession` refuses a cellar lot outright); the duplicated auto-select block became `pickSessionLot` in `lotUtils` — **best usable jar, else the oldest usable CELLAR lot — so the chosen lot is guaranteed BY CONSTRUCTION to be one the picker will list**, which is what makes the display/state divergence impossible rather than merely unlikely; `canIgnite` requires a lot that RESOLVES among the usable ones (not merely a non-empty `lotId`, since a setup persisted across relaunches can point at a lot finished since); and the auto-end says `tasting_err_autoend_failed` when the save did not happen. **The gate and the message are both needed** — one stops it happening, the other stops the app lying about it if it ever does. Three smaller ones in the same pass: the auto-end **no longer navigates** (`addSessionFromTasting(form, {navigate:false})`) because it fires from a timer while the user is elsewhere, possibly mid-form, and a direct `nav()` bypasses `formGuardRef` — only `goBack` consults it; the tasting's failed cellar-open now REPORTS itself (`tasting_open_err`) instead of silently dropping the pending state, which its `SessionFormView` twin has always done; and `fmtLotWeight` gives an absent weight ONE rendering (`—`) where the app had three (`0g` on the lot row — asserting a figure the user never gave — `—` in the detail modal, and a hole in the picker label), keeping `0g` for a lot genuinely weighed at zero so the distinction stays visible on screen. `tobaccoHasUsableLot` is also live in production at last: exported, unit-tested and DEAD since it was written, the `tobaccoHasTag` shape that knip cannot see because a test counts as a consumer. **Probed five ways; the third probe came back GREEN and was VACUOUS** — it prefixed `false &&` onto the branch while the test asserts the SOURCE contains `auto && !ok && setSaveError`, so the substring survived. Redone by deleting the branch outright, it reddens. Same lesson as the two probes above: **a probe that does not change what the assertion actually reads is not a probe.**

**FOUR readers of the untracked-weight notion were fixed and there were SIX, and the fifth one turned the fix into a silent data loss.** An audit of the whole session mechanic (live + manual), each finding re-verified before acting. **(a) THE FIFTH READER: the three weight CAPS in `useSessionStore`.** `_persistSession` and both branches of `updateSession` clamp the recorded weight to what the lot holds (`Math.min(w, safeW(lot.weightG))`) — a guard against recording more than exists — and `safeW("")` is 0, so every session logged against a lot that fix had just made offerable **recorded 0 g**. Worse than the bug it followed: the tobacco now appears in the picker, the session saves, the journal shows it, and the grams are gone with no message. **Un-correctable by editing**, since the edit path clamps the same way. All three now exempt an untracked lot — the cap protects a KNOWN balance, and there is nothing to protect when there is no balance. **(b) THE SIXTH READER: a local COPY of the predicate in `InventoryDetailView`** (`(parseFloat(l.weightG)||0) > 0`), which the fix left behind — so the fiche went on printing « renseigne le poids d'un lot pour pouvoir utiliser ce tabac dans une séance » about a tobacco both pickers were already offering. The copied-predicate failure this repo keeps paying for (the tag predicate ×4, `FAMILY_AGING_MAX`, `CATS`). **The hint is KEPT, not deleted, and REWORDED in all six languages**: the state that still warrants it is an explicit ZERO — a weight typed by hand, or a finished lot reopened at 0 — which the pickers do refuse, and « renseigne le poids » was the wrong sentence for it because the weight IS filled in. Both halves of that reversal are pinned (zero raises it, unweighed does not), on the assertion itself so it is not "fixed" back. **(c)** The cellar auto-select fallback was duplicated verbatim in `SessionFormView` and `TastingView` and read the raw weight too; both go through `isUsableLot` now. **(d) A CROSS-CHANNEL defect, found while reading the same area and unrelated to weights: the storage-full warning could be disarmed for SEVEN DAYS by a gesture that has nothing to do with it.** `saveWarn` is a SHARED channel — the tasting auto-end notice was put on it, and the `save()` QuotaExceeded migration has been on it far longer — while the banner's × wrote `cave-quota-warn-dismissed` **unconditionally**. So closing « Dégustation clôturée automatiquement après 95 min » silenced « le stockage est à 80 %, sauvegardez avant que les écritures échouent » for a week, silently. The dismissal moved into `useStorageQuotaWarning` gated on the same `raisedRef` the clearing branch has: **the hook is the only place that knows whether the banner on screen is ITS banner**, so the write belongs there and nowhere else; the banner now REPORTS the dismissal (`ctx.dismissQuotaWarn`) and a second close cannot re-arm it. Probed in all four directions, each mutation verified applied and reddening exactly its own cases.

## La porte cassée par la prose qui l'expliquait

**Vingt minutes après avoir écrit « une porte qui cesse de voir son sujet ne rapporte RIEN », j'ai cassé la porte 9 en écrivant exactement cela.** L'index de `CLAUDE.md` explique ce que fait la porte et le fait en la citant : « gate 9 still slices the tree out of `## Repository Structure` wherever that section now lives ». ## Douze règles que rien ne gardait, et elles sont toutes du CÂBLAGE

Le découpage de `CLAUDE.md` avait un but : rendre les sous-agents de sondage viables. Trois ont été relancés aussitôt après, sur des cibles étroites (≤ 4 règles chacun, sonde par CONSÉQUENCE contre la suite entière) — `SettingsModal`, les modales Courses / Comparer / Recherche, et les quatre hooks de démarrage. **Les trois ont tenu, là où trois sur quatre étaient morts avant. Douze règles sondées, DOUZE non gardées.**

**LE MOTIF EST UN SEUL, ET IL EST DÉJÀ NOMMÉ DANS CE DÉPÔT : la logique est éprouvée là où elle est DÉFINIE et non gardée là où elle est APPELÉE.** `cloudEncryptionWiring` couvre le chiffrement, `useImportConfirm` la fusion, `imgGcGating` le ramassage de photos, `progressiveList` la fenêtre progressive — tous bons. Ce que rien ne testait, c'est **quel bouton appelle quoi, avec quel argument, sous quelle condition**. Un moteur juste derrière un fil croisé se comporte exactement comme un moteur cassé. C'est la septième et la huitième instance de cette forme (après `chooseAutoSaveTarget`, `reDeductRestoredSessions`, `findParityGaps`, `useUnsavedFormGuard`).

**La plus chère : la carte « Fusionner ».** Recâblée sur `applyImport("replace")`, la suite entière reste verte — et l'ancienne suite `SettingsModal.test.tsx`, 92 cas, reste verte elle aussi (vérifié séparément). La carte dont le texte promet « Rien n'est effacé » effacerait la cave entière, sans confirmation et sans corbeille : la corbeille garde des suppressions unitaires, pas un remplacement de base. Les deux cartes sont à trente lignes l'une de l'autre et se ressemblent trait pour trait ; un seul mot les sépare.

Les onze autres, par coût décroissant : **« Importer la sélection » sans son `selectedSet`** (on coche 2 tabacs sur 400, on en reçoit 400, et une fusion ne s'annule pas) ; **`useOrphanPhotoGC` qui ne visite pas les accessoires** (chaque photo d'accessoire effacée d'IndexedDB au lancement suivant — `imgGcGating` couvre tabacs/pipes/envies mais toutes ses données portent `accessories: []`) ; **l'interrupteur de chiffrement dans ses deux sens** (l'éteindre sans confirmation envoie les sauvegardes en clair ; l'allumer sur une invite ANNULÉE laisse « chiffrement actif, phrase absente », état dont `cloudEncryptionWiring` mesure lui-même l'effet : l'auto-save ne monte plus rien, sans message) ; **`useProgressiveList` qui ne débranche pas l'observateur précédent** (les `IntersectionObserver` s'empilent, la fenêtre double à chaque coup d'œil, et le gel de 13 s revient par la porte de derrière — le faux observateur du test existant a un `disconnect` no-op, donc par CONSTRUCTION il ne peut pas voir l'empilement) ; **`useLotIntegrityProbe` qui recompte** (le compteur de diagnostic grimpe seul et annonce une corruption qui s'aggrave alors que rien n'a bougé) ; **la sourdine de 7 jours de `useStorageQuotaWarning` qui n'expire pas** (les cas existants ne posent que des sourdines datées de MAINTENANT : ils vérifient qu'elle fonctionne, jamais qu'elle prend fin) ; **la relecture de `cave-shopping-checked` au montage** (l'écriture était gardée, la relecture non : en magasin, un rechargement fait disparaître les coches) ; **`copyList` qui copierait les lignes cochées** (on rachète ce qu'on vient d'acheter) ; **le `setCatalogSeed(q)` d'un hit catalogue** (atterrissage en haut d'un catalogue non filtré, la correspondance qu'on venait de voir introuvable) ; **la croix de `CompareModal`** (elle nomme sa colonne, donc elle promet — et le test existant ne cliquait qu'avec UNE colonne, où toute croix retire la bonne : la règle n'était vérifiable qu'à partir de deux).

**DEUX MUTATIONS ONT ÉTÉ ÉCARTÉES COMME ÉQUIVALENTES, ce qui est la moitié honnête du protocole** : le plafond `COMPARE_MAX` est déjà tronqué en amont par `buildComparison`, et le filtre `deletedAt` de `CompareModal` reçoit `liveData`, déjà filtré — les retirer ne change rien en production. Une survivante n'est une trouvaille que si la mutation change vraiment le comportement.

**CE QUE J'AI VÉRIFIÉ MOI-MÊME plutôt que sur rapport**, parce qu'un agent qui se trompe se trompe avec aplomb : les trois affirmations principales, chacune en opposant l'ANCIENNE suite à la NOUVELLE sous la même mutation — 92 cas verts contre 1 rouge pour la fusion, 17 contre 2 pour les coches de courses, 28 contre 1 pour les photos d'accessoires. Et une correction dans deux des trois fichiers rendus : les fixtures nommaient des mélanges RÉELS ; remplacés par le vocabulaire inventé du dépôt.

**RÉSIDU ÉNONCÉ, dans les mots des agents** : `GDriveConfirmPanel` (choix de la sauvegarde à restaurer) et `SyncDiagView.onDeleteEntry` (suppression d'un fichier cloud ligne à ligne) sont deux surfaces destructrices du même fichier, non sondées faute de budget. Et une remarque de conception qui vaut mieux qu'un test : `applyImport(mode)` sans second argument signifie « tout prendre » — une signature dont l'ABSENCE d'argument est le comportement le plus large est un défaut par défaut.
